# Framework Selection Notes

Use these frameworks as lenses with bounded applicability. Combine only the parts that improve the working model; never present the collection as a universal formula.

## Selection guide

| Need | Useful lens | Contribution | Main caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explain how evidence and interpretation interact | Data/Frame sensemaking | Elaborate, question, preserve, or replace a frame as data changes | A coherent frame can still be wrong |
| Understand an evolving operational state | Situation awareness | Perceive relevant elements, comprehend meaning, project near-future status | Perception is selective and projections are uncertain |
| Structure an actual choice | Decision Quality | Test frame, alternatives, information, values, reasoning, and commitment | A good process cannot guarantee a good outcome |
| Choose a method appropriate to the context | Cynefin | Distinguish ordered, expert-analyzable, complex, chaotic, and confused contexts | Classification can become false certainty |
| Model expert action under pressure | Naturalistic / recognition-primed decision-making | Use recognition and mental simulation when expertise and time pressure matter | Intuition is unreliable without valid experience and feedback |
| Examine dynamics and unintended consequences | Causal or influence mapping | Expose mechanisms, feedback, delays, thresholds, and leverage points | An arrow is a hypothesis, not proof of causality |
| Stress-test a proposed action | Premortem and scenario analysis | Surface failure modes, branching futures, signposts, and mitigations | Vivid stories can be mistaken for probabilities |

## Data/Frame sensemaking

Use when the problem is not merely missing data but uncertainty about what the data mean. A frame can be a story, map, script, hypothesis, plan, or causal explanation. It selects salient data and connects them; new data may elaborate, question, or break the frame.

Procedure:

1. Name the current frame and the anchors that evoked it.
2. Identify what the frame explains and predicts.
3. Search for anomalies, residue, and low-quality anchors.
4. Elaborate the frame if new evidence fits.
5. Compare a genuinely different frame if anomalies persist.
6. Name evidence that would discriminate between frames.

Starting sources:

- [Naturalistic Decision Making Association: Data/Frame model](https://naturalisticdecisionmaking.org/new-ndm-tools/)
- [National Academies discussion of Data/Frame sensemaking](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/13188/chapter/5)

## Situation awareness

Use for a world in motion. Separate:

1. **Perception** — relevant elements, attributes, and changes.
2. **Comprehension** — their significance relative to goals and each other.
3. **Projection** — likely future states, conditional on actions and uncertainty.

Add observables and update triggers so the model can be revised. Do not confuse the state of understanding with the process used to obtain it.

Starting source:

- [NASA technical report summarizing Endsley's model](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20020034930/downloads/20020034930.pdf)

## Decision Quality

Use only after declaring a real decision. Inspect six linked requirements:

1. a helpful frame;
2. creative, feasible alternatives;
3. useful and sufficiently reliable information;
4. clear values and tradeoffs;
5. sound reasoning about consequences and uncertainty;
6. commitment and ability to follow through.

Improve the weakest decision-relevant link rather than seeking perfect information everywhere. Keep values separate from information: facts do not decide what a person should care about.

Starting sources:

- [Decision Education Foundation: defining Decision Quality](https://www.decisioneducation.org/principles-of-decision-quality/defining-decision-quality)
- [Strategic Decisions Group: Decision Quality](https://sdg.com/decision-quality/)

## Cynefin

Use as a context diagnostic, not as a complete analysis:

- **Clear/ordered:** apply known practice while watching for oversimplification.
- **Complicated:** analyze or consult expertise; more than one good answer may exist.
- **Complex:** probe safely, observe emergent patterns, and adapt; prediction is limited.
- **Chaotic:** stabilize immediate harm, observe the response, then reassess.
- **Confused:** decompose the situation or gather enough orientation to classify parts.

Different parts of one situation may occupy different domains, and the domain can change.

Starting source:

- [Official Cynefin overview](https://cynefin.io/wiki/Cynefin)

## Naturalistic decision-making

Use when stakes, time pressure, team constraints, dynamic conditions, and expertise dominate. Experienced decision-makers may recognize a familiar pattern, generate a plausible action, and mentally simulate it rather than compare a long option list. Test whether the experience came from an environment with repeatable patterns and timely, accurate feedback. When it did not, prefer explicit analysis, experiments, or external expertise.

Starting sources:

- [Naturalistic Decision Making principles](https://naturalisticdecisionmaking.org/ndm-principles/)
- [Gary Klein: Recognition-Primed Decision model](https://www.gary-klein.com/rpd)

## Causal maps and scenarios

Use a causal map when relationships, feedback, or delays are central. Label each link as observed, supported, inferred, or disputed. Include direction, delay, thresholds, and feedback only when meaningful. Generate counterfactuals: if a presumed cause changed, what should follow?

Use scenarios when more than one future is plausible. Build branches from two or three decision-relevant uncertainties, not from imaginative detail. For each scenario, identify early signposts, consequences, robust moves, contingent moves, and exit conditions. Do not attach numerical probabilities without evidence.

For a premortem, assume the chosen course failed and reconstruct credible causes, then convert them into prevention, detection, response, or recovery measures. Keep success and opportunity scenarios alongside failure analysis to avoid one-sided threat framing.

Starting source:

- [Gary Klein: Performing a Project Premortem](https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem)

## Research discipline

Research the world, not just framework names. Start from gaps that could alter the model or decision. For each material claim record:

- claim and source;
- observation date or effective date;
- source type and relevant interest;
- whether it is direct evidence, synthesis, or interpretation;
- confidence and conflicting evidence;
- which part of the model changes if the claim is false.

Prefer primary records for events, rules, specifications, and stated positions. Use systematic reviews, official guidance, or peer-reviewed research for general effects. Use reporting and stakeholder accounts to reveal context and dispute, not as automatic ground truth. Search for disconfirmation and missing affected groups before stopping.
