# Decision Brief

Make the choice understandable and explain what each option would cause next.

## Frame the actual choice

Identify the decision, who owns it, the desired outcome, constraints, and any real deadline or trigger. Use the user's stated priorities. Mark assumptions instead of inventing objectives, authority, or urgency.

Read the relevant response, artifact, or task. If the choice is already settled, report the recorded decision and its basis; do not reopen it without a reason. If there is no decision to make, say so and provide orientation or a next action rather than manufacturing options.

## Compare viable options

Present only materially different, feasible options. Include waiting, gathering evidence, or keeping the current approach when these are genuine alternatives. Scale detail to the stakes; a small table usually suffices.

For each option, explain its benefit, cost or downside, dependencies, reversibility, and practical consequence where those factors change the choice. Distinguish established facts from estimates and preferences. Research changing or uncertain material facts using authoritative sources when needed; name gaps that could reverse the recommendation. Do not assign invented probabilities, costs, or delivery dates.

Recommend one option when the evidence supports it, explaining the tradeoff in the user's terms. If uncertainty prevents a useful recommendation, identify the smallest check or experiment that would change the choice. For deeper situation analysis, use `model-the-situation` when available and needed; routine decisions should remain concise.

## Make the next step explicit

State the exact choice needed, the recommended option, and what work follows if it is selected. Give the owner and location of the first follow-on action. Do not phrase the recommendation as an already made decision or turn “looks useful” into broad execution permission.

Preparing a brief does not choose, execute, or persist anything by itself. If asked to save the brief or decision, use [checkpoint](../checkpoint/SKILL.md) and [the shared record contract](../checkpoint/references/work-records.md). Keep an unresolved choice as Kind: Decision with the options and recommendation visibly pending. Record an actual choice with its owner, date, rationale, and source, using the same task ID. Link a substantial brief from that entry rather than creating an independent decision backlog.

Once a choice and resulting work are already authorized, proceed within that scope without requesting the same approval again. Keep unresolved external actions separate from local recording.
